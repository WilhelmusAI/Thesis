## 3. Assess conditional plausibility before researching outcomes

First assess plausibility using the original event, preceding generated consequences and established knowledge available at the information cutoff.

Treat earlier generated consequences as hypothetical premises when assessing the next transition. Do not reject a later consequence merely because an earlier consequence did not happen historically.

However, accepting preceding consequences does not automatically justify the next consequence.

Record the initial plausibility assessments before retrieving post-event outcome evidence. Do not claim that this ordering was followed unless it actually was. If outcome evidence has already been seen, disclose that limitation.

Apply the following definitions without substituting a different scale.

### Rating 1 — Strongly disagree

At least one transition directly conflicts with an applicable premise or established constraint. The conflict remains even when preceding generated consequences are accepted as hypothetical assumptions.

Examples:

- A payment is explicitly said to occur after receipt of an invoice, but its specified date precedes the invoice date.
- The scenario states that a loan’s interest rate is fixed throughout the period, but the branch predicts that a policy-rate change directly changes that contractual rate during the same period.

Identify the exact conflicting statements or constraint.

### Rating 2 — Disagree

No decisive contradiction warrants Rating 1, but at least one transition cannot reasonably be justified without a substantial unsupported assumption or a necessary missing mechanism.

Examples:

- A modest reduction in interest expenditure is said to eliminate every source of a company’s default risk, without addressing other obligations or business risks.
- A reduction in mortgage applications is said to cause unrelated corporate borrowers to receive credit-rating downgrades, without explaining a connecting exposure or mechanism.

Identify the substantial assumption or essential missing mechanism. Do not assign Rating 2 merely because a consequence is uncertain or does not necessarily follow.

### Rating 3 — Unsure

No identified failure warrants Rating 1 or 2, but ambiguity or insufficient information prevents a confident judgment about at least one essential transition.

Examples:

- An existing loan is predicted to reprice next month, but its fixed or variable status and reset date are unspecified.
- Currency depreciation is predicted to increase an importer’s payment, but the invoice currency and relevant exchange-rate protection are unspecified.

Explain which missing information would resolve the uncertainty.

### Rating 4 — Agree

Every transition is reasonably plausible under the stated assumptions. No essential reasoning link is missing, although some mechanisms remain implicit and rely on standard background knowledge.

Examples:

- A permitted variable-rate loan reset increases repayments, reduces disposable income, prompts a discretionary purchase to be postponed, lowers expected retail sales and leads to smaller replenishment orders.
- A port closure delays a shipment, exhausts the stated limited stock buffer, creates a stockout, prompts some customers to choose available substitutes and reduces sales of the unavailable product.

A plausible consequence need not be inevitable. Ordinary mechanisms need not all be explained from first principles.

### Rating 5 — Strongly agree

Every transition is reasonably plausible and clearly justified. Relevant mechanisms, temporal ordering and necessary conditions are specified without contradiction or an essential reasoning gap.

Examples:

- A contractual loan reset leads to a correctly specified instalment calculation, a larger required payment, an explicit household budget adjustment and a corresponding reduction in planned discretionary spending.
- A shipment delay exceeds specified inventory coverage, creates a dated stockout, triggers an available substitution option, prompts customers with stated compatible requirements to switch and changes the product mix of sales.

### Plausibility aggregation

Apply the first applicable definition from Rating 1 upwards across the branch. A defective transition cannot be offset by stronger reasoning elsewhere.

Identify the determining transition and sentence. If several transitions determine the same rating, identify the earliest and list the others where useful.

Keep these distinctions clear:

- Missing historical evidence does not make a transition implausible.
- Uncertainty about whether an outcome will occur does not itself justify Rating 2.
- Rating 3 represents an indeterminate assessment.
- Ratings 4 and 5 do not require proof that the sequence happened.
- Historical contradictions must not automatically replace an independently assessed plausibility rating.

## 4. Research historical evidence

After recording plausibility assessments, research the predicted developments within the run’s observation window.

Search for both supporting and contradicting evidence. Prefer official statistics, original institutional publications, company disclosures, exchange records and other primary sources. Use reliable secondary reporting where appropriate.

Open and inspect sources used as evidence. Search snippets alone are insufficient for a final factual finding.

For each source, distinguish:

- Publication date.
- Date or period of the reported observation.
- The exact fact established.
- Which predicted component it addresses.
- Relevant limitations.

Later publications may verify observations that occurred within the window. A publication released within the window may describe earlier events and therefore fail to verify a new prediction.

Do not treat whole-quarter or whole-month statistics as exact matches for narrower dates or populations without adequate justification.

Do not select whichever market subperiod happens to agree with a prediction. Follow its specified timing and comparator. If these are ambiguous, record the ambiguity and use a consistent declared convention.

Use public evidence appropriate to the claim. Do not automatically require private customer records or internal documents when official notices, contractual terms or other adequate evidence establish the relevant component. Conversely, do not assume that undocumented internal actions occurred.

An explicitly claimed causal connection requires evidence addressing that connection. Mere co-occurrence is insufficient. Clearly attribute causal explanations offered by sources and distinguish them from established observations or your own inference.

## 5. Historical evidence categories

### Supported

Evidence establishes every required component of the proposed sequence, including its explicit qualifications. No component is contradicted or remains unresolved.

Examples:

- Dated notices, contractual terms, calculations and transaction records establish a named bank’s rate increase, customer notification, instalment recalculation, revised billing and subsequent payment, including the asserted connections.
- Records establish a shipment cancellation, activation of a replacement clause, replacement dispatch, additional freight payment and delivery on the predicted date.

### Partially supported

Evidence establishes at least one required component, while at least one other component remains unresolved. No required component is contradicted.

Identify both the supported and unresolved components.

Examples:

- A bank’s rate increase and specified repayment changes are confirmed, while subsequent household spending, retailer ordering and supplier production changes remain unresolved.
- An airline’s fuel-cost increase and surcharge are confirmed, while the predicted booking decline and flight reductions remain unresolved.

Partial support does not mean that most of the branch happened. A branch with one confirmed component may qualify.

### Refuted

Evidence establishes that at least one required component conflicts with documented developments or fails an explicit qualification under the predefined matching rules.

This verdict takes precedence over partial support.

Examples:

- A branch predicts an exact 50-basis-point increase, while the named institution documents an exact 25-basis-point increase for the same decision.
- A branch predicts no trading in a specified asset on a specified exchange during an exact interval, while authenticated records show executed trades during that interval.

Absence of evidence alone must never produce Refuted.

### Not supported

Available evidence establishes neither support nor contradiction for any required predicted component.

Identify the evidence limitation.

Examples:

- A branch describes five internal company decisions, but no available records establish or contradict any of them.
- A branch describes an unidentified fund’s portfolio and hedging actions, but no sufficiently specific transaction or holdings evidence is available.

This category does not mean that the branch is false.

## 6. Evidence-matching safeguards

Apply the same standards throughout the run.

- Preserve the entity, population, instrument, date and measure when matching evidence.
- Do not substitute estate-agent satisfaction for buyer sentiment, general confidence for spending, applications for completed loans, or an auction schedule for successful issuance.
- Do not count a broad thematic resemblance as a supported component.
- A supported subcomponent must be genuinely asserted in the original prediction, not created by removing its defining subject or meaning.
- Background facts and root-event repetitions do not establish new predicted outcomes.
- Separate the policy rate, commercial prime rate, loan interest rate, bond coupon and market yield.
- Do not invent numerical tolerances for words such as “approximately,” “slightly” or “significantly.”
- If wording has multiple reasonable interpretations that materially change a verdict, flag the ambiguity and report the sensitivity. Do not silently choose the interpretation producing a preferred result.
- Distinguish an unresolved claim from a confirmed contradiction.

Do not target a particular distribution of grades. Zero fully supported branches is permissible. Do not raise or lower scores to make the results appear more balanced or useful.
